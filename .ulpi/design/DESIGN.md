## Design read

An industrial field manual for young technical builders: engineering paper, measured rulers, depth markers, pixel display type, and one accent per theme.

## Direction

Committed direction: industrial and signage. The engineering-paper canvas, the rules, the technical labels and one accent make the subject legible without borrowing a game world or a generic SaaS style.

## Counterfactual test

The identity depends on the field-manual concept, the pixel display type, the Ore currency icon and the Digital and Physical Loadout loop. If a screen could belong to any tech programme, it has failed.

## Brief rules

- Each brief states its surface, status, components, interaction, responsive behaviour and accessibility constraints.
- A brief is a record. Only the design system and the code are current. An implemented or superseded brief does not authorize a repeat change.
- New briefs go in `.ulpi/design/` and are listed in that folder's README.

## Product truth to keep in every brief

- The four lifetime tracks are Tools, Systems, Compute and Hardware.
- Research Mode is a modifier, not a fifth track.
- Ore is global and spendable. Track XP is separate.
- Shipped work grows the Digital Loadout. Rewards upgrade the Physical Loadout.

## Named anti-slop bans

A direction fails if it uses any of these without a brief-backed reason: purple or blue glow or gradients as decoration; gradient text; glassmorphism; soft, oversized SaaS panels; generic stock or AI "industrial" art; fake dashboards; repeated equal-card grids; cards nested inside cards; and decorative bolts or rivets on every surface.

The beige paper is the light theme's identity, not a generic cream. A beige that is not the design system's paper fails.
