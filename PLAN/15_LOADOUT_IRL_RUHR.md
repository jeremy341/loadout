## 1. Purpose and boundary

LOADOUT may later run a physical Germany build weekend connected directly to the online YSWS. The event should make the Digital Loadout / Physical Loadout idea tangible while using the existing online program, review, tracks, economy, and Community Era rules.

This is post-online-launch work. It is not needed for the initial online MVP. Event attendance must not become necessary for online progression, and attending must not grant an event-only Track XP, Bolt, level, or permanent advantage. Event projects use the normal eligibility, evidence, review, attribution, and award rules.

## 2. Connect the event to the same LOADOUT Eras

The event uses the **same shared global Era** as online participants. It does not create an IRL-only history, Era meter, reset, or progression track.

- Continue using the four LOADOUT tracks: **Tools, Systems, Compute, Hardware**. Research Mode is an optional project modifier, not a fifth track.
- Approved event projects contribute to the normal global Era Points total under Plan 11. Their points are not spendable currency or Track XP.
- Apply the existing Era build-bonus rule without an event-specific variant: a separate reviewer decision determines whether a project qualifies for the planned **+10% of its approved base Bolt award**. It adds **no Track XP** and does not change the four quality dimensions.
- Keep the Era active for at least 14 days. Reaching its threshold makes it **ADVANCEMENT READY**; transition occurs only at the next eligible weekly reset under Plan 11.
- An event contribution does not force an Era transition on Sunday. If the threshold is crossed, the event can show that the next Era unlocks at the next eligible reset.

## 3. Location strategy

**Preferred region: NRW / Ruhrgebiet.** Every named place below is a research target only; no venue, partner, date, access, capacity, price, or agreement is confirmed.

1. **Essen** — first-choice concept: Zollverein or another industrial venue in/around the Zollverein complex. It best combines Ruhr location, access, industrial identity, and LOADOUT's technical theme.
2. **Duisburg** — alternative concept: Landschaftspark Duisburg-Nord, with a strong industrial setting.
3. **Bochum** — especially viable if a university or technical partner can provide an affordable practical venue.
4. **Düsseldorf** — useful transport/airport access, though less directly connected to the industrial theme.

If NRW proves impractical, **Hamburg** is the current non-NRW alternative because of its engineering, port, logistics, and industrial identity.

A practical university/workspace may host the build itself while an industrial landmark is used only for an opening, tour, sponsor activity, or final demo. The build venue must be evaluated for youth-event rules, overnight access, power, reliable networking, insurance, safeguarding, sleep arrangements, accessibility, transport, and total cost before selection. Do not infer that an industrial landmark is suitable for overnight hacking.

## 4. Format: three calendar days

The recommended format is Friday through Sunday. “SHIFT” is a restrained field-manual label, not an instruction to work without sleep.

### Friday — SHIFT 01 / IGNITION

- arrivals and check-in;
- opening and clear program/safety explanation;
- current Era context and reveal, if approved and configured;
- track exploration and team formation;
- initial building time.

### Saturday — SHIFT 02 / PRODUCTION

- main build period;
- technical workshops and mentors;
- hardware/maker resources where safely staffed;
- checkpoints and optional side challenges;
- community activities and real rest periods.

### Sunday — SHIFT 03 / SHIP

- final build period;
- documentation and benchmarking;
- submissions and expo/demo floor;
- normal project review/judging arrangements;
- optional awards;
- configured global Era contribution reveal;
- closing.

Do not describe the event as a 72-hour no-sleep hackathon. Detailed timetable, start/end times, quiet hours, overnight policy, supervision, and participant support remain operational decisions.

## 5. Tracks and project areas

Use the same track definitions as the online program. Zones, signs, mentors, workshops, and equipment may help builders find relevant support, but participants need not stay physically isolated by track.

- **Tools:** debugging, profiling, developer tooling, measurement tooling, automation.
- **Systems:** servers, runtimes, networking, databases, protocols, operating systems, self-hosting.
- **Compute:** GPU compute, ML systems, graphics, simulation, FPGA compute, optimization, parallel computing.
- **Hardware:** microcontrollers, PCBs, electronics, robotics, sensors, fabrication, test equipment.

Projects may span tracks under normal LOADOUT XP-allocation rules. Reviewers allocate XP to the work actually shipped. Research Mode remains a modifier for technical questions, experiments, reproductions, benchmarks, or investigations; it is not an alternate event track.

## 6. Awards and review

Avoid making the weekend only a generic first/second/third-place contest. Optional award concepts include:

- Best Tools Build
- Best Systems Build
- Best Compute Build
- Best Hardware Build
- Best Era Build
- Best Technical Depth
- Best Research / Measurement
- Best Overall Loadout

These are options, not a required award list. Define a fair, published judging approach before the event if any are used. Awards must not change the global Bolt/XP economy without a separate approved rule. Normal YSWS review remains the source of project eligibility and participant progression; an award does not silently guarantee a project payout.

## 7. Digital and Physical Loadout on site

**Digital Loadout:** show participant projects, track progress, technical artifacts, benchmarks, and shipped work through an event dashboard or display only when privacy and display permissions are settled.

**Physical Loadout:** a staffed demo/loan area may show sponsor or organizer equipment such as development boards, soldering equipment, logic analyzers, test equipment, FPGA boards, mini PCs, GPUs, 3D printers, or fabrication tools. Availability, supervision, safe use, ownership, and return handling need separate operational plans.

The event story is:

> **Your projects become your Digital Loadout. Your rewards upgrade your Physical Loadout.**

Displaying an item does not mean it will be given away. Do not advertise equipment, prize quantities, sponsor support, or participant take-home kits until confirmed.

## 9. Scale and readiness gates

The current planning range is approximately **60–100 builders**. It is not committed capacity. Optimize the first event for a technically strong, well-supported weekend rather than maximum attendance.

Do not proceed to event launch/booking until organizers have evidence that:

- the online project → review → progression loop works reliably;
- peer/project reviews and participant support are reliable;
- the Bolt/XP economy and reward fulfillment are stable enough;
- there is meaningful German/European participant demand;
- staffing and organizer workload are sustainable;
- venue requirements, safeguarding, youth-event rules, insurance, accessibility, travel and sleeping arrangements are understood;
- realistic funding/sponsorship and regional fulfillment have been secured;
- any Hack Club/YSWS relationship, branding, program status, and use of funds is accurately represented and approved.

No threshold in this checklist substitutes for required legal, safeguarding, venue, funding, or Hack Club confirmation.
