Once we've accepted your ship, you'll get two rewards: **Ore** to spend in the shop and **Track XP** that levels up the fields you worked in. You'll spend the Ore when you buy something, but the XP stays. As your level goes up, it gives you a better discount on gear in that track, including gear you buy later.

## What's the difference between Ore and XP? {#balances}

| What you earn | What happens to it |
| --- | --- |
| Ore | Goes into your balance. Buying a prize spends some of it. |
| Track XP | Goes into the tracks used by your work. It stays when you buy something. |

There's also [Era Points](/docs/eras), which track the community's shared progress toward the next theme. They don't go into your shop balance or personal levels.

## How an hour becomes Ore {#work}

First, we check which of your hours we can approve. Other builders then review the project to decide its **Ore multiplier**. Once you have those two numbers, you can work out the base award:

```text
base Ore = approved hours × Ore multiplier
```

For example, **10× means 10 Ore per approved hour**, so ten approved hours earn 100 Ore. Normal rates run from **1× to 20×**, with a target mean of 10×. A project that reaches Great Signal can earn up to 30× per approved hour. Here's what the same ten hours earn at different rates:

| Ore multiplier | The work | Ten approved hours earn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1× | Lower end of the normal review curve. | 10 Ore |
| 5× | A below-average result on the normal curve. | 50 Ore |
| 10× | The target mean of the normal scale. | 100 Ore |
| 15× | An above-average result on the normal curve. | 150 Ore |
| 20× | The normal hourly ceiling. | 200 Ore |
| 30× | Great Signal ceiling only. | 300 Ore |

You don't choose a row when you submit. These examples show what different review results would pay for the same ten hours. Reviewers look at Originality, Technical Depth, Execution and Documentation; [Project review](/docs/review#quality) explains how the multiplier is set. A ship that passes the team's checks can still receive a low multiplier; a rejected ship earns nothing.

If the ship also qualifies for the [Era bonus](/docs/eras#bonus), add 10% of its base Ore award. A 100-Ore base award becomes 110 Ore. This is separate from the quality multiplier and adds no Track XP. How it combines with future Ore bonuses is still undecided.

## How an hour becomes Track XP

Reviewers award Track XP for the work and split it between the tracks you actually used. Designing a board and building a substantial service for it can contribute to Hardware and Systems, but the two tracks share one award. Listing both doesn't double your XP.

You might now be wondering whether a 10× ship gives you ten XP per hour too. We haven't settled the XP equation or how quality will affect it, so don't use the Ore multiplier to predict an exact XP award. We do have a target for how long it should take to reach the top level, though.

## Your field levels {#levels}

Each track has **15 lifetime levels**. LV.15 is **3,500 Track XP**, with a target of about **350 approved hours of solid work in one track**. If you split the work equally between two tracks, reaching LV.15 in both is a target of about 700 hours altogether.

Your permanent discount on related gear rises with your track level, reaching **20% at LV.15**. Along the way, levels **3, 6, 9, 12 and 15** each give you a [Field Requisition](/docs/requisitions) for an extra discount on one larger purchase.

We're still setting the intermediate XP thresholds and discount percentages. The milestones above don't imply a published percentage at every level, and leveling up doesn't raise the Ore multiplier for your next ship. That comes from its own review.

## Do my levels ever reset? {#what-stays-with-you}

No. Your Track XP, levels, accepted projects and Digital Loadout stay with you across Seasons and Era changes. Buying something spends Ore from your balance, but it won't take away the progress you made to earn it.

A Season can start a fresh competitive ranking while you keep your lifetime progress. An Era changes the community's shared theme. Neither sends your tracks back to level one.

## Where progress pays off

When you're ready to spend Ore, [Pricing](/docs/pricing) shows how your level affects the total. A [Requisition](/docs/requisitions) can take another 20% of the base price off an eligible item, and [Custom Orders](/docs/custom-orders) explain how to request something outside the catalog.
