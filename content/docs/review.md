When you send in your project, the team checks that it fits LOADOUT and that we can verify the work and hours you're claiming. Then we accept the ship or send it back with a short written reason. Accepted ships earn **10 Ore per approved hour**, plus Track XP.

## Who checks my project? {#stages}

| Stage | Who | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Check | The team | Opens your files and demo, checks authorship and verifies the eligible hours. |
| Decision | The team | Accepts the ship, or sends it back with a short written reason. |
| Payout | The system | Adds Ore and Track XP for an accepted ship. We can hold a payout while something is checked. |

We check every ship the same way. If your demo doesn't open or the board files are missing, we'll ask you to fix that first, so the decision rests on the project itself.

If we send a ship back, read the reason before rebuilding everything. It might be a dead demo link or a missing file, and fixing that may be all it takes.

## What we look at {#quality}

We look at four things. The written reason says which of them mattered for the decision:

| Dimension | What we look for |
| --- | --- |
| Originality | The idea or approach you contributed, including how you worked around unusual constraints. |
| Technical Depth | The difficult problems you tackled and your understanding of the solution. |
| Execution | Whether the project does the job you claim, including the behavior you've tested. |
| Documentation | Whether somebody can understand, inspect or reproduce the work. |

Take the [sensor-board example](/docs/shipping#example). The circuit and firmware show what the builder implemented. The comparison with a reference thermometer backs up the temperature claim, while the stated limits stop a room-temperature test from becoming a claim about every environment.

These four things explain the decision. They don't set a score, a multiplier or a payout tier.

## How much does a ship earn?

Accepted ships earn a fixed **10 Ore per approved hour**. Ten approved hours earn 100 Ore. The rate doesn't depend on how the team judges the ship, so you can work out the payout for your hours in advance. [Ore, XP & levels](/docs/progress#work) has the details.

Payout only comes from accepted ships. The timing of payouts is still being set.

## Cheating {#fraud}

Fake or padded hours, copied work presented as your own, and invented evidence are fraud. The consequence is a **permanent ban, with everything earned or bought taken back**. There is no second chance.

If something took longer than expected, explain the problem and keep the record of the attempts. An honest failed experiment is something we can discuss; invented progress isn't.

## AI use and team projects {#authorship}

Give credit where the work appears. A teammate's firmware, a vendor driver and an AI-assisted parser have different authors, and we should be able to tell them apart. [AI & team contributions](/docs/ai-teams) shows what to disclose without turning your submission into a list of every conversation you had.

## Feedback and revisions {#feedback}

What if we send a ship back? The written reason explains the problem and what we'd need to see to check it again. Fix the problem, add any new journal entries and send it back in. Your hours stay in your records while you do that.

When you ship a new version instead, describe the changes and include the new journal entries. That version gets its own decision; we don't pay for the same hours twice. See [Shipping again](/docs/shipping#versions) for the minimum new work.

## Questions about a decision

If you think we missed something, send the ship's reference, the part you disagree with and the evidence to [#loadout-help](https://app.slack.com/client/E09V59WQY1E/C0C6XBLM0M7). We haven't decided on a formal appeal route yet, but the team will look at what you send.
