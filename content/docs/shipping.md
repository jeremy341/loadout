A ship is a submitted project version, not simply a time total. Another builder should be able to understand what changed and inspect the result.

## From an idea to a ship {#steps}

1. Choose a technical capability and the relevant track or tracks.
2. Build it while recording your own work and decisions.
3. Prepare a usable result with source or design files and supporting evidence.
4. Explain the version, contribution, limitations, and any assistance.
5. Submit for fit, validity, and quality review once submissions are available.

> **Submissions are not open.** These are intended evidence expectations, not instructions for an existing submission portal.

## Software evidence

Provide the relevant source, how the result is run or inspected, and a demonstration when useful. Explain dependencies, important versions, and known limitations.

Checks, measurements, or reproducible outputs should support the capability you claim. A polished screenshot alone does not show that a runtime, tool, or algorithm works.

## Hardware evidence

Provide relevant design files, firmware, schematics, CAD, or build documentation. Photos and a demonstration should show the actual device and what it does.

Explain which parts you designed, what you assembled, and what you tested. Identify unfinished or simulated behavior rather than presenting it as a working physical result.

## Tracking and journals {#journal}

Connect tracked work to decisions, experiments, failures, and improvements. [Tracking & evidence](/docs/tracking) describes that relationship; time recording does not guarantee approval.

## Shipping another version {#versions}

A later ship should identify genuinely new work. Explain the difference from the earlier accepted version and submit only the new contribution.

The same implementation or hours cannot be rewarded twice. [Project review](/docs/review) explains the review stages.
