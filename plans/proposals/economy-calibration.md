## 2. Assumptions to confirm first

These two decisions drive every price below.

1. **A solid project earns 10 Ore per approved hour.** This is the owner's rate, and it matches Plan 02, which says a normal good project lands around 10×. It is also the proposal's yardstick for "hours of effort".
2. **1 Ore is worth about $0.50 of program cost.** That makes a solid hour worth $5 of prizes, a little under the $5.50 an hour that is common in YSWS programs. A builder at level 15 who uses a Requisition gets about $8.33 an hour, so progress is what lifts the rate above the common one. This is an **internal budgeting figure only**. Plan 02 says never to promise a public conversion such as 1 Ore = $X, and that stays true. Change it and every base price scales with it. Eligibility is settled: high-school students aged 13 to 18, the same window Stardance uses.

## 3. Proposals

### 3.1 The discount curve

Formula: **discount = round(20% × ((level − 1) ÷ 14)^1.2)**. It bends only a little, gains about 1.4 points a level, and hits exactly 20% at level 15. The bend is small on purpose, so focus is rewarded without punishing builders who work in two areas (section 3.8).

| Level | Permanent discount |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 1% |
| 3 | 2% |
| 4 | 3% |
| 5 | 4% |
| 6 | 6% |
| 7 | 7% |
| 8 | 9% |
| 9 | 10% |
| 10 | 12% |
| 11 | 13% |
| 12 | 15% |
| 13 | 17% |
| 14 | 18% |
| 15 | 20% |

Plan 02's older provisional curve topped out at 15%. This replaces it.

### 3.2 How the savings combine

There are no limits in Ore. The permanent discount and the Requisition apply in full at every price.

- The **permanent discount** is the percentage from 3.1 times the item's base price.
- A **Requisition** saves 20% of the base price. It needs an item of at least **400 Ore**, so it can't be spent on small things.
- Both use the base price, so they add up, and the most anyone saves is **40%**.
- Rounding: every saving is rounded to the nearest whole Ore.

Worked examples:

- **1,000 Ore item, level 15, with a Requisition:** 1,000 − 200 (20% permanent) − 200 (20% Requisition) = 600 Ore.
- **A bigger item:** 1,800 base: permanent saving 360 and Requisition 360 → 1,080 Ore, the same 60% of base.

### 3.3 Which discount applies, and buying outside your field

- **Items related to several tracks use your best matching discount, never the sum.** SSD (Systems and Compute), base 160 Ore. Builder: Compute level 10 (12%), Systems level 4 (3%) → uses 12% (the better one, not the sum) → 141 Ore.
- **Items outside your tracks cost a little more.** The proposal is a **+10% markup** on the base price for builders with no progress in any related track. Raspberry Pi 5, base 200 Ore. A builder with no progress in Hardware or Systems pays 220 Ore (base plus 10%).
- Most prizes stay open to everyone. The two special items (the enclosed printer and the Framework 16) have no level requirement either: they are priced at $3 an hour instead of $5, so one hour of work buys 60% as much of them. The other Specialist items (FPGA board, oscilloscope and bench supply, Framework-class laptops) still carry a level requirement, and Mastery items need level 9 or higher in a related track. A discount or a Requisition never unlocks a level requirement.

### 3.4 XP and levels

**Award.** XP measures practice more than polish, so it follows the hours first and the quality second:

**Track XP = approved hours × (10 + quality multiplier) ÷ 2.**

At the baseline of 10× that is 10 XP an hour, exactly the same as the Ore. Quality counts for half as much in XP as it does in Ore, so a weak project still builds some skill, a great one levels you faster, and nobody can level by luck of the draw alone. Ore is spendable; XP is not. The reviewers then split that one XP total across the tracks the work used. The split must add up to the total, so the same work can never earn XP twice.

| Hours | Multiplier | Ore | Track XP |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 5× | 50 | 75 |
| 10 | 9× | 90 | 95 |
| 10 | 10× | 100 | 100 |
| 10 | 15× | 150 | 125 |
| 10 | 20× | 200 | 150 |

- A track gets a share only if its share is at least 10%. Smaller shares go to the main track.
- A 30-hour project at 14× gets 420 base Ore and 360 Track XP. The reviewers split the XP 70% Compute and 30% Systems: Compute +252, Systems +108. The total never exceeds 360.
- **Research Mode:** an accepted, reproducible report adds **25% to that project's XP**, split the same way. It adds no Ore.
- **Lifetime:** XP and levels never reset, whatever the Season or Era.

**Levels.** Cumulative XP needed per track, from the formula **3,500 × ((level − 1) ÷ 14)^1.6**, rounded to the nearest 50. "Solid-project hours" divides by 10. Level 15 is set at 3,500 XP, about 350 solid hours, so mastery in one track is realistic within a season or two of steady work. A builder who splits their work over two areas reaches level 15 in both after about 700 hours.

| Level | Total XP in the track | Solid-project hours to get there |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 50 | about 5 |
| 3 | 150 | about 15 |
| 4 | 300 | about 30 |
| 5 | 450 | about 45 |
| 6 | 650 | about 65 |
| 7 | 900 | about 90 |
| 8 | 1,150 | about 115 |
| 9 | 1,450 | about 145 |
| 10 | 1,750 | about 175 |
| 11 | 2,050 | about 205 |
| 12 | 2,400 | about 240 |
| 13 | 2,750 | about 275 |
| 14 | 3,100 | about 310 |
| 15 | 3,500 | about 350 |

What this means in effort: level 5 (the first Requisition) is about **45 hours** of solid work in one track. Level 8 is about **115 hours**. Level 10 is about **175 hours**. Level 12 is about **240 hours**. Level 15, mastery, is about **350 hours**, or about 250 hours if the work is rated great (14×). A builder who spreads work over several tracks levels each of them more slowly, which is the point.

### 3.5 Ore payouts and peer review

**Formula.** Base Ore = approved hours × quality multiplier. The multiplier is a number from 0 to 20 that peers decide, and **10 is the default for a solid project**. Ten hours at 10× is 100 Ore. Ten hours at 9× is 90 Ore, so you lose 10. Ten hours at 20× is 200 Ore. Then the Era bonus, if it applies, is added: +10% of the base. Bonuses are **added**, never multiplied together.

**Approved hours** count in quarter hours, and only the hours the team approves count (section 3.9). A partial approval pays for the approved part only, at the multiplier for that part.

**How peer review works.**
1. You ship a project. A team member checks that it is real: it runs or can be shown, the evidence and journal are there, and the hours add up. This is the same first gate Stardance uses. Nothing is scored until it passes.
2. **Seven** builders are picked at random to review it, preferring people with some level in a track the project uses. A project with fewer than five completed reviews waits.
3. Each reviewer scores **four categories from 0 to 20**: Originality, Technical Depth, Execution and Documentation. They also propose how the work splits across tracks.
4. For each category the **median** of the reviewers' scores is taken, so one very high or very low vote cannot move it. The multiplier is the **mean of the four medians**, rounded to one decimal.
5. After a 24-hour window in which the team can stop a payout over fraud flags, the Ore and XP are issued.

**What the numbers mean (anchors).** Reviewers score against written anchors with an example project at each, not against each other, so scores do not drift with who happens to be in the pool:

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Nothing there, or not the builder's own work |
| 5 | Weak: it exists but is unfinished or unclear. The lowest a team-approved ship pays. |
| 10 | Solid: works, shows real effort, and is explained. **The default.** |
| 15 | Great: clearly above what most builders ship |
| 20 | Exceptional: you would show this to an expert |

A ship the team approves pays at least **5× per hour**, even if peers score it lower, so a genuinely weak project still pays 50 Ore for ten hours. A project that is not approved pays nothing. Reaching 20× needs the median of all four categories at 20, which is meant to be rare but possible.

**Keeping reviews honest.**
- **You review to ship.** Each ship needs three completed reviews from you first, so there are always reviewers (Stardance does the same with its vote balance).
- A score of 4 or lower, or 16 or higher, needs a one-line reason.
- A reviewer who is often far from the medians counts for less over time, and one who is clearly careless or collusive is removed. Reviewing your own project, a teammate's, or swapping scores is fraud.
- If the program average drifts well above 10, the anchors are tightened. The prices are not touched.

**Payout examples.**

| Project | Approved hours | Medians (O, T, E, D) | Multiplier | Base Ore | With Era bonus (+10%) | Track XP |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A small first ship | 8 | 5, 6, 6, 7 | 6× | 48 | 53 | 64 |
| A solid weekend build | 20 | 10, 10, 10, 10 | 10× | 200 | 220 | 200 |
| A strong month of work | 30 | 14, 13, 15, 14 | 14× | 420 | 462 | 360 |
| An exceptional long project | 60 | 18, 17, 16, 17 | 17× | 1,020 | 1,122 | 810 |

**Rounding.** Ore and XP are rounded to the nearest whole number after the multiplier. The same hours or the same work are never paid twice. Fraud is a full ban (section 3.8).

**Bonuses in the first program.** Only the Era bonus. The Signal bonus, referral, usage milestones, open-source bonus and achievements in Plan 02 wait until the base economy is steady.

### 3.6 Custom Orders

| Tier | Level needed | Largest request (base Ore) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Field | 4 | 400 |
| Power | 8 | 1,200 |
| Root | 12 | 3,000 |
| Bare Metal | 15 | 6,000 |

- **Tracks:** the item's related tracks. You need the tier's level in **any one** of them.
- **Limits:** one open request at a time, and at most two approved Custom Orders per builder per Season.
- **What a request must include:** a supplier link, key specs, how it ties to a project or a technical need you can show, and which track it fits.
- **Quote:** the team's landed cost (item, shipping, tax and handling) × 2 Ore per dollar, rounded up to the next 5 Ore, held for 14 days. The quote, not your estimate, is the price.
- **Discounts:** your permanent discount applies to the quote as in 3.2. A Requisition applies only if the quote is marked eligible and meets the 400 Ore minimum.
- **Cancellation and refunds:** you can decline a quote at no cost. After you accept, you can cancel free until the team places the order with the supplier, and the Ore is refunded in full. After that, Ore is refunded only if the program cancels or delivery fails, and a spent Requisition is restored in the same cases.

### 3.8 Balance rules

**Decided by the owner (2026-10-15):** Ore and XP carry across seasons. There is **no limit on hours** per season, per week or per project: if someone does 1,000 or 2,000 legitimate hours, they are rewarded for all of them. Money is treated as not limiting for now, so the rules below are about fairness and abuse, not cost.

**Specialization pays, but only a little.** Level 15 takes about 350 solid hours in one track. A builder who works in two areas still reaches level 15 in both, after about 700 hours. The bend in the discount curve is small, so focus is a head start, not a trap:

| Builder | Ore earned | Level (one track) | Discount | Level (spread over two tracks) | Discount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 hours | 400 | 4 | 3% | 3 | 2% |
| 100 hours | 1,000 | 7 | 7% | 5 | 4% |
| 200 hours | 2,000 | 10 | 12% | 7 | 7% |
| 300 hours | 3,000 | 13 | 17% | 9 | 10% |
| 350 hours | 3,500 | 15 | 20% | 10 | 12% |
| 500 hours | 5,000 | 15 | 20% | 12 | 15% |
| 700 hours | 7,000 | 15 | 20% | 15 | 20% |

Spread over four tracks, a builder is at 300 hours: level 6, 6%; 700 hours: level 10, 12%. The best-match rule (never the sum) stays, so a generalist cannot add small discounts together.

**Requisition schedule (a change for the owner to confirm).** The owner agreed five identical tickets per track at levels 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15. Tickets are plentiful but a builder spends at most one per order, so the first ticket is the one that matters, and at level 3 it would arrive after about 15 hours as a flat 20% coupon. Proposal: earn them at **levels 5, 8, 10, 12 and 15** (about 45, 115, 175, 240 and 350 hours). They stay identical, one per order, 20% of the base price, and never expire.

**Keeping rates solid without hour limits.**
- The team approves hours, not the tracker's total, and peers score quality (section 3.5). Hours without a matching journal and evidence are not paid, and nothing is paid twice.
- **Fraud is a full ban.** Fabricated or inflated hours, copied work, fake evidence, or any other fraud ends in a permanent ban from LOADOUT, with the Ore, XP and any Requisition it earned or bought taken back. There is no second chance.
- A new ship of the same project is reviewed on its own window (section 3.9).

**Reviewer guidance.** A typical teenage project should land around 8 to 10, with 10 for solid work. If reviews average well above 10, the anchors are tightened (section 3.5), not the prices.

**Why prices, not caps, keep it solid.** Savings stay unlimited as the owner chose. The prices, the 400 Ore Requisition minimum and one ticket per order carry the balance. The two special prizes are priced at $3 an hour, so the biggest purchases stay far away even for a maxed builder.

### 3.9 Shipping again

A project is not finished when it first ships. You can keep working, keep your journal and tracked time, and **ship the same project again, as many times as you like**. This follows Stardance, where each ship pays for the work since the previous one.

- **Each ship covers its own window:** the hours logged since the previous ship. Hours already paid are never paid again, and nothing earlier is changed or taken back, except for fraud.
- **A ship needs new work:** at least **5 approved new hours**, journal entries for them, and a short note saying what changed. A ship with no visible change is not reviewed.
- **Each ship is peer reviewed on its own,** with the earlier work visible as context. The reviewers score what this window added, so a polish pass is not rated as if it were the whole project. Scores can be higher or lower than last time.
- **Ore and XP come from that window only.** Example: a first ship of 20 hours at 9× pays 180 Ore and 190 XP. A second ship 3 weeks later of 12 hours at 13× pays 156 Ore and 138 XP.
- **A rejected ship can be fixed and sent again.** The hours stay in the same window and are not lost.
- **Each ship counts as a project for review duty:** you owe three reviews for every ship, first or later.
- There is no limit on the number of ships, in line with the owner's decision that hours are not limited.
