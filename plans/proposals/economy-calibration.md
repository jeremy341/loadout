# Economy calibration: proposals for the owner to edit

**Status:** PROPOSED, drafted 2026-10-15. Nothing here is approved, and nothing here is published. [Plan 02](../02-economy-and-rewards.md) stays the single authority. When the owner approves a value, it moves into Plan 02 as **Agreed**, the conflicting older text is removed (Plan 19, workstream G), and only then can the Docs and homepage use it.

Every number below is generated by a small script from the rules in section 2 and section 3, so changing one rule changes the tables consistently. The examples are invented. No price, stock or brand here is a promise.

## 1. What is already agreed

These come from the owner and are the fixed inputs.

| Rule | Decision |
|---|---|
| Permanent discount | Progress in a related track gives an ongoing discount that reaches **20% at level 15**. |
| Field Requisitions | **Five identical tickets per track**, earned at **levels 3, 6, 9, 12 and 15**. No I, II or Master versions. |
| Requisition saving | An extra **20% of the item's base Ore price**. The permanent discount applies alongside it, and both use the base price. |
| Limits | One Requisition per order. One use, no expiry, not transferable. It must match the item's track. Level requirements still apply. If the program cancels an order, the ticket is restored. |
| Era bonus | **+10% of the approved base Ore award**, from a separate yes or no reviewer decision. No Track XP bonus. |
| Your example | 1,000 Ore − 20% permanent − 20% Requisition = 600 Ore, "where caps permit". |

Check: 1,000 − 200 (20% permanent) − 200 (20% Requisition) = 600 Ore. It works, so long as the caps in section 3.2 allow it.

## 2. Assumptions to confirm first

These two decisions drive every price below.

1. **A solid project earns 11 Ore per approved hour.** Plan 02 says a normal good project lands around 10×, with 9× to 12× called solid. 11× is the middle of that band. It is also the proposal's yardstick for "hours of effort".
2. **1 Ore is worth about €0.50 of program cost.** That makes a solid hour worth about €5.50 of prizes, in line with other YSWS programs. This is an **internal budgeting figure only**. Plan 02 says never to promise a public conversion such as 1 Ore = €X, and that stays true. Change it and every base price scales with it.

## 3. Proposals

### 3.1 The discount curve

Formula: **discount = round(20% × (level − 1) ÷ 14)**. It rises steadily, gains about 1.4 points a level, and hits exactly 20% at level 15.

| Level | Permanent discount |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0% |
| 2 | 1% |
| 3 | 3% |
| 4 | 4% |
| 5 | 6% |
| 6 | 7% |
| 7 | 9% |
| 8 | 10% |
| 9 | 11% |
| 10 | 13% |
| 11 | 14% |
| 12 | 16% |
| 13 | 17% |
| 14 | 19% |
| 15 | 20% |

Plan 02's older provisional curve topped out at 15%. This replaces it.

### 3.2 Savings limits

A percentage is dangerous on expensive items, so each discount has a limit in Ore. Proposed:

| Base price (Ore) | Permanent discount limit | Requisition limit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Up to 1,000 | none | 300 Ore, or 20% if smaller |
| 1,001 to 2,000 | 250 Ore | 300 Ore, or 20% if smaller |
| 2,001 to 4,000 | 350 Ore | 300 Ore, or 20% if smaller |
| Over 4,000 | 450 Ore | 300 Ore, or 20% if smaller |

- The **permanent discount limit** stops the 20% from growing without bound on big items. Up to 1,000 Ore there is no limit, which is why your example comes out at 600.
- The **Requisition limit** is one shared number for every item: **300 Ore**. A Requisition saves 20% of the base price, or 300 Ore, whichever is smaller.
- A Requisition needs an item of at least **400 Ore**, so it can't be spent on small things.
- Rounding: every saving is rounded to the nearest whole Ore.

Worked examples:

- **1,000 Ore item, level 15, with a Requisition:** 1,000 − 200 (20% permanent) − 200 (20% Requisition) = 600 Ore.
- **A bigger item:** 1,800 base: permanent saving 250 (20% would be 360, but the limit is 250), Requisition 300 (20% would be 360, limit 300) → 1,250 Ore. This is why a Requisition is not a promise of 20%: the caps decide.

### 3.3 Which discount applies, and buying outside your field

- **Items related to several tracks use your best matching discount, never the sum.** SSD (Systems and Compute), base 160 Ore. Builder: Compute level 10 (13%), Systems level 4 (4%) → uses 13% (the better one, not the sum) → 139 Ore.
- **Items outside your tracks cost a little more.** The proposal is a **+10% markup** on the base price for builders with no progress in any related track. Raspberry Pi 5, base 200 Ore. A builder with no progress in Hardware or Systems pays 220 Ore (base plus 10%).
- Most prizes stay open to everyone. Only Specialist items carry a level requirement, and Mastery items need level 9 or higher in a related track. A discount or a Requisition never unlocks a level requirement.

### 3.4 XP and levels

**Award.** Track XP for a project equals its **base Ore** (approved hours × quality multiplier). Ore is spendable; XP is not. The reviewer then splits that one XP total across the tracks the work used. The split must add up to the total, so the same work can never earn XP twice.

- A track gets a share only if its share is at least 10%. Smaller shares go to the main track.
- A 30-hour project scored 12 gets 465 base Ore and 465 Track XP. The reviewer splits it 70% Compute and 30% Systems: Compute +326, Systems +139. The total never exceeds 465.
- **Research Mode:** an accepted, reproducible report adds **25% to that project's XP**, split the same way. It adds no Ore.
- **Lifetime:** XP and levels never reset, whatever the Season or Era.

**Levels.** Cumulative XP needed per track, from the formula **7,000 × ((level − 1) ÷ 14)^1.6**, rounded to the nearest 50. "Solid-project hours" divides by 11.

| Level | Total XP in the track | Solid-project hours to get there |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 100 | about 10 |
| 3 | 300 | about 25 |
| 4 | 600 | about 55 |
| 5 | 950 | about 85 |
| 6 | 1,350 | about 125 |
| 7 | 1,800 | about 165 |
| 8 | 2,300 | about 210 |
| 9 | 2,850 | about 260 |
| 10 | 3,450 | about 315 |
| 11 | 4,100 | about 375 |
| 12 | 4,750 | about 430 |
| 13 | 5,450 | about 495 |
| 14 | 6,200 | about 565 |
| 15 | 7,000 | about 635 |

What this means in effort: level 3 (the first Requisition) is about **25 hours** of solid work in one track. Level 6 is about **125 hours**. Level 9 is about **260 hours**. Level 12 is about **430 hours**. Level 15, mastery, is about **635 hours**, or about 450 hours if the work is rated great (15.5×). A builder who spreads work over several tracks levels each of them more slowly, which is the point.

### 3.5 Ore payouts

**Formula.** Base Ore = approved hours × quality multiplier. Then the Era bonus, if it applies, is added: +10% of the base. Bonuses are **added**, never multiplied together.

**Approved hours** count in quarter hours, and only the hours a reviewer approves count. A partial approval pays for the approved part only, at the multiplier for that part.

**The four-dimension rubric.** Each dimension (Originality, Technical Depth, Execution, Documentation) is scored from 0 to 4. 0 is nothing there, 1 basic, 2 solid, 3 strong, 4 exceptional. The four scores add up to 0 to 16, and the multiplier is **2 + 1.125 × the total**, rounded to the nearest half. That spans Plan 02's 2× to 20×.

| Total of the four scores (0 to 16) | Quality multiplier | Rough label |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 2× | weak or incomplete |
| 2 | 4.5× | okay |
| 4 | 6.5× | okay |
| 6 | 9× | solid |
| 8 | 11× | solid |
| 10 | 13.5× | great |
| 12 | 15.5× | great |
| 14 | 18× | exceptional |
| 16 | 20× | exceptional |

**Payout examples.**

| Project | Approved hours | Scores (O, T, E, D) | Multiplier | Base Ore | With Era bonus (+10%) | Track XP |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A small first ship | 8 | 1, 1, 1, 2 | 7.5× | 60 | 66 | 60 |
| A solid weekend build | 20 | 2, 2, 2, 2 | 11× | 220 | 242 | 220 |
| A strong month of work | 30 | 3, 3, 3, 3 | 15.5× | 465 | 512 | 465 |
| An exceptional long project | 60 | 4, 4, 3, 4 | 19× | 1,140 | 1,254 | 1,140 |

**Rounding, revisions and partial approvals.**
- Ore and XP are rounded to the nearest whole number after the multiplier.
- A revised ship is re-reviewed. If the new award is higher, you receive the difference. If it is lower, you keep what you were already paid, unless fraud is found.
- The same hours or the same work are never paid twice.

**Bonuses in the first program.** Only the Era bonus. The Signal bonus, referral, usage milestones, open-source bonus and achievements in Plan 02 wait until the base economy is steady.

### 3.6 Custom Orders

| Tier | Level needed | Largest request (base Ore) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Field | 4 | 400 |
| Power | 8 | 1,200 |
| Root | 12 | 3,000 |
| Bare Metal | 15 | 6,000 |

- **Tracks:** the item's related tracks. You need the tier's level in **any one** of them.
- **Limits:** one open request at a time, and at most two approved Custom Orders per builder per Season.
- **What a request must include:** a supplier link, key specs, how it ties to a project or a technical need you can show, and which track it fits.
- **Quote:** the team's landed cost (item, shipping, tax and handling) × 2 Ore per euro, rounded up to the next 5 Ore, held for 14 days. The quote, not your estimate, is the price.
- **Discounts:** your permanent discount applies to the quote with the same limits as in 3.2. A Requisition applies only if the quote is marked eligible and meets the 400 Ore minimum.
- **Cancellation and refunds:** you can decline a quote at no cost. After you accept, you can cancel free until the team places the order with the supplier, and the Ore is refunded in full. After that, Ore is refunded only if the program cancels or delivery fails, and a spent Requisition is restored in the same cases.

What this shows:
- Small prizes (under 400 Ore) get the full 20% from level 15 and no Requisition. They cost 2 to 27 solid hours.
- Mid-priced prizes (400 to 1,000 Ore) are where a Requisition matters most: a 500 Ore printer drops from 45 solid hours to 27 at level 15 with a ticket, a 40% saving.

**Exposure, worst case.** 200 builders × 30 approved hours × 11 = **66,000 Ore** issued. At €0.50 per Ore that is about **€33,000** of prizes. Discounts do not create Ore, but they let each Ore buy more: if every builder reached level 15 and used a Requisition on everything, which would not happen, value per Ore rises by at most about 40%, so the ceiling is about **€46,200**. The real figure will be far lower because most builders will not reach level 15, and discounts are capped on expensive items. Sponsor subsidy and region are left out.

## 4. What this changes in Plan 02

Once the owner approves, workstream G removes or rewrites these conflicting parts:

1. Section 26.5: the provisional curve topping out at 15%.
2. Sections 26.8 and Part III rules 11 and 12: the I, I, II, II, Master tickets, and their +200 B, +500 B and 1,200 B values, become five identical Field Requisitions worth 20% of base, with the shared limit.
3. Section 26.6: the cap bands are rescaled to the Ore scale in 3.2.
4. Sections 11 and 26 onward: the leftover "B" unit from the old currency name becomes Ore, and example prices are rescaled to section 2's assumptions.
5. Sections 26.11 and 26.12: General Requisitions are not covered by the owner's decision. They stay as drafted until the owner decides whether to keep them.
6. The Docs page on Requisitions drops the I, II and Master names, and the homepage progression table follows.
